# AlphaGenome — architecture overview (original model; not AlphaGenome 2)
Source: Avsec et al. Advancing regulatory variant effect prediction with AlphaGenome. Nature 649, 1206–1218 (2026). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10014-0
Figure scope: sequence-to-function inference backbone and all output types. Training, teacher distillation, variant scoring and benchmarking are out of this architecture overview.
Input: one-hot encoded 1,048,576-bp DNA sequence plus human/mouse species identity. The U-Net-inspired backbone contains a convolutional encoder with pooling from 1-bp to 128-bp resolution, a transformer tower at 128 bp for long-range sequence dependencies and pairwise representation updates, and a convolutional upsampling decoder with matched encoder skip connections restoring 1-bp resolution. Output embedder representations include 1D embeddings at 1 and 128 bp and 2D pair embeddings at 2,048 bp. Species-specific prediction heads produce 5,930 human or 1,128 mouse tracks across 11 output types. 1-bp output types: RNA-seq, CAGE, PRO-cap, DNase, ATAC, splice sites, splice site usage, splice junctions. 128-bp outputs: histone modifications and transcription-factor binding. 2,048-bp outputs: DNA contact maps. Splice junction count prediction uses donor–acceptor pairs from 1D embeddings rather than ordinary independent position-wise linear heads. Diagrams of tracks and contact maps are symbolic readout examples, not empirical results. Sequence parallelism over eight TPU v3 devices is an implementation detail omitted from this conceptual view, not a claim that the full long sequence runs on one device.
Scientific invariants: preserve the encoder-to-decoder skips; keep pair/contact readout distinct from high-resolution 1D tracks; do not present expression or disease as generated biological outcomes; this is a prediction model, not a DNA generator. No biological cell-condition input or condition adaptor is added: that would conflate the original model with AlphaGenome 2.
