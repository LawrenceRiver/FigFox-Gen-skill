## Scientific source
Qwen-Music methodology architecture overview, grounded in arXiv:2607.11699v3 Sections 2 and 3. Text-to-song and cover generation share a music-token language model and an acoustic renderer. A prompt rewriter expands a user description into music tags and lyrics, which condition both the autoregressive model and renderer. Qwen-Music-LLM is initialized from a 3B dense Qwen3.5-Omni variant. Original-song generation may optionally predict a Melody-CoT before full-mixture Music Semantic Tokens; plain direct text-to-token generation is also supported. Cover generation instead supplies reference melody tokens in the prefix. A distinct Melody Tokenizer uses 50 Hz RMVPE vocal pitch, 8x median pooling, relative MIDI centering and a 256-entry vocabulary to form a 6.25 Hz contour representation; it is not the music codec. Music tokens are rendered with a text-conditioned flow-matching DiT, Spec-VAE decoder, Band-Mode Refiner and inverse STFT to produce 48 kHz stereo audio. A frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B text encoder supplies description features and a trained lyric transformer supplies lyric features for DiT cross-attention. The broad illustration may group these as text conditioning. Qwen-Music-Tokenizer supplies 25 Hz discrete music representations from log-Mel features via a causal Conformer prefix and a single 32768-entry codebook. Its four training stages are BestRQ, causal adaptation, multi-task supervision and VQ insertion. The deployed tokenizer discards the upper post-VQ training layers and supervision heads. It supplies offline music representation and LM training targets; it is not a mandatory audio-input stage for text-only generation. LLM training uses quality-graded pretraining followed by supervised cold start, iterative offline DPO and online GSPO. The figure emphasizes inference and the representation interfaces; training recipes stay compact and visually distinguished from inference. No evaluation scores or benchmark plots belong in this diagram.
